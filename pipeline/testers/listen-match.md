A listener who only heard the narration once, without the pictures, wrote:
"{{LISTENING}}"

Did the voice alone make it clear? Check four things:
- About: the listener knows what the video is about.
- What happened: the listener has the main events and why they matter, with nothing that changes their meaning.
- People: everyone central to it is someone the listener can place (who they are, not just a name). A side name they couldn't place doesn't count.
- Followed: nothing central confused them. A jump between lines they couldn't connect, or a key term they didn't know, is central.

Their own words are fine, and a small detail left out is fine. Judge what they understood, not what they wished the video had said: a question about a detail the video didn't give (an exact date, a currency, a price, a precise place) is never a reason for WRONG, on any line. If their "what happened" has the main events, What happened is RIGHT.

Write four short lines (About: RIGHT or WRONG, What happened: RIGHT or WRONG, People: RIGHT or WRONG, Followed: RIGHT or WRONG), then a last line: VERDICT: YES if all four are RIGHT, otherwise VERDICT: NO.
