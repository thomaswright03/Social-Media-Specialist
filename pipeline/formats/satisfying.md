# Format: satisfying AI video (entertainment)

An oddly satisfying loop-friendly video: perfect fits, smooth
transformations, precise motion. The picture and the sound carry it. It
never sells, and the caption says it was made with AI.

<!-- ideas -->
Format: a satisfying AI-made video. It is entertainment: no selling, no product, no business.

What you are pitching: one simple, tactile process a viewer can't stop watching, about 35 seconds long, with no voice and no story. It is drawn as flat vector animation, one shot at a time, so it has to be built from solid shapes making simple moves: sliding, dropping, rolling, stacking, slotting, sorting, turning, splitting apart.

People watch these to the end because they can see what "finished" will look like and need to see it get there. Each small step lands exactly, and each landing promises the next. So the pitches that win share a shape:
- One process on one surface (a table top, a studio floor, a plain backdrop).
- A goal the viewer can see from the first second: an outline to fill, a grid with empty places, a stack that isn't square yet.
- A chain of small fits, each a little bigger, faster or more exact than the last.
- One doubt: it isn't obvious the last piece will fit, or what the finished thing will turn out to be.
- A last, biggest fit that completes it. Then it returns to how it began, so it loops.

A pitch is one sentence that names the process and its last, best moment: "Forty marbles roll down a spiral track and drop one by one into a grid until the last one completes a perfect smiley."

The kind of thing that works: a piece fits exactly, a shape snaps into place, colours sort themselves, a pile becomes a perfect stack, a ball runs a track and lands in the one hole, a block splits into perfect slices. These show the kind of thing; they are not the list. A process nobody has seen beats a familiar one done again.

Test every pitch before you offer it:
1. Shapes test. Can every step be drawn with circles, boxes and simple outlines that slide, drop, roll or turn? Liquids, sand, cloth, smoke, slime and anything that pours or squashes can't be drawn well this way. Leave them out.
2. Goal test. Would a stranger, with the sound off, know from the first frame what it is building toward?
3. Chain test. Does it break into ten or more small steps that each finish cleanly?
4. Last-moment test. Is the final fit clearly the biggest, and can one picture show it?
5. Loop test. Can it end where it started?

Not a story: no characters, no faces, no dialogue, no twist.

<!-- script -->
Format: a satisfying AI-made video. It is entertainment: no selling, no product and no business, in any field.

How this video is made, which is the reason for the rules below: there is no voice. Each shot is one flat vector drawing with a small motion, drawn by an artist who may see that shot alone. The sound effects are made from each shot's `sound`.

The examples in these rules show the kind of thing meant. Do not reuse them.

The process:
- One process, on one surface, with a goal the viewer can see from the first frame.
- Each shot is ONE motion that starts, runs smoothly and ends on a clear fit the drawing can show: the piece slides in flush, the ball drops into its hole, the stack squares off. Something lands in every shot.
- It escalates. Each fit is a little bigger, faster or more exact than the last, building to the biggest and cleanest in the second-to-last shot. That fit is the `payoff`.
- The last shot returns everything, in one motion, to how the first frame looked, so the video loops.
- Only solid shapes making simple moves: sliding, dropping, rolling, stacking, slotting, turning, splitting apart. Nothing that pours, flows or squashes.

No narrator:
- Every shot's `line` and `onScreen` are empty.

The sound:
- The sound is the star. Every shot has one precise cue in `sound` that lands on the moment of the fit, saying what it is made of and how it sounds: "a soft wooden click", "a glassy tap", "a deep felt thunk".
- The cues grow with the fits. The biggest fit gets the biggest sound.

The drawings (`visual`):
- Every `visual` stands on its own. Say what is on the surface, how many, their shapes and colours, and where they are.
- Give the count at the start and the end of every shot ("7 of 12 slots are filled; the eighth block slides into the top-right slot"), so that separate drawings agree on what has already happened.
- Describe the motion as from where to where, and say what the fit looks like when it lands.
- Shapes and colours stay the same from the first shot to the last. Name them the same way every time.
- No characters, no hands, no faces, no text in the world.

The camera:
- `camera` is exactly one of: wide, medium, close-up, over-the-shoulder, low angle, high angle, insert. This format uses mostly close-up, insert and high angle.
- high angle: straight down on the surface. Use it for grids and patterns.
- close-up: one fit, filling the frame.
- insert: the single piece or slot that matters next.
- wide: the whole thing so far. Use it every few shots so the viewer sees it growing, and for the biggest fit.
- low angle: along the surface, for stacks and things that rise.
- Change the camera on every cut, and use at least three kinds.

On-screen text:
- The opening carries its hook, as stage 3 requires.
- After that, at most 3 words, and only where it adds tension ("ONE LEFT", "PERFECT FIT?"). Most shots have none.

Other fields:
- `place` is the one surface, named the same way in every shot.
- `story` says in two sentences what process the viewer watches and how it ends.
- The caption says it was made with AI ("Made with AI"), asks one light question ("Which one was the most satisfying?"), then has 3 to 5 hashtags including #satisfying.

Clean, tactile, 3D-looking flat vector, like a product render. Each shot is watched on a phone for two or three seconds, and the pleasure is precision: the viewer has to be able to see that things line up exactly.

Objects:
- Smooth solid shapes with perfect geometry: exact circles, true parallel lines, equal gaps, everything aligned to a grid.
- Volume comes from a soft gradient across each object, plus one small bright highlight on the side the light comes from. Edges are crisp.
- Each kind of object keeps its exact size, shape and colour in every shot.

Surface and light:
- One surface, a matte table top or a seamless studio floor, filling the frame edge to edge.
- Soft light from above, with a soft shadow under every object where it touches the surface. An object in the air has its shadow below it, smaller and fainter.

Palette:
- The background is a warm off-white or a deep slate. The look sheet picks one and gives its hex.
- The objects use 3 or 4 of these saturated candy colours: coral #ff6b6b, teal #2ec4b6, butter #ffd166, violet #8b5cf6.
- Lime #a3e635 is saved for the final, perfect fit. It appears nowhere before it.

Never: characters, hands, faces, or text in the world.

Framing, by camera:
- high angle: straight down. The surface is flat to the frame, and grid lines run true vertical and horizontal.
- wide: the whole arrangement, centred between y 330 and y 1250, with surface around it.
- close-up: one fit fills the frame, with the gap that is closing at the centre.
- insert: one piece or one slot, huge.
- low angle: along the surface, with the objects rising against the backdrop.

Motion:
- Motion is the point. Every moving part travels on a smooth ease-in-out and comes to rest exactly in place.
- Everything lines up exactly at the end of the shot.
