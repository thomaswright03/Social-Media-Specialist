# Format: product ad (20% of Reels)

One product from wright-ai-solutions.com, and Wright AI Solutions itself: we
build custom AI agents and software for businesses.

<!-- ideas -->
Format: an ad. It markets ONE product (below) and Wright AI Solutions as a whole: we build custom AI agents and software for businesses.

What you are pitching: a tiny story, about 35 seconds long, told by a narrator over stick-figure drawings. It has to entertain first. A viewer who will never buy should still enjoy watching, because a viewer who scrolls past never reaches the product. The pitches that win share a shape:
- One business owner, someone the viewer could be or could know.
- One painful, specific problem with a cost: a missed call that lost a job, a pile of invoices at midnight. Not "too much admin".
- The product fixing it with one real feature, in a way that looks almost unbelievable.
- The owner's day visibly different afterwards.

A pitch is one sentence with the owner, the problem and the moment the product saves them. It names the product by its exact name.

Test every pitch before you offer it:
1. Wince test. Someone who runs a business recognises the problem at once and feels what it cost.
2. One-feature test. One real feature of this product, from what is given below, does the saving.
3. Honesty test. Nothing in the pitch is something the product can't do, and no result is claimed that the product's page doesn't claim.
4. Stranger test. Someone who doesn't own a business would still enjoy it as a small story with a turn.

Pitches that fail:
- A list of features. One problem, one feature.
- A problem that needs explaining before it hurts.
- A made-up customer praising the product. That is an invented review.

Format: an ad. It markets ONE product (below) and Wright AI Solutions as a whole: we build custom AI agents and software for businesses. Name no other product.

It has to entertain first. A viewer who will never buy should still enjoy it, because a viewer who scrolls past never reaches the product.

How this video is made, which is the reason for the rules below: one narrator's voice plays over a sequence of drawings, one per shot, each with a small motion. The characters are white stick figures in a drawn workplace. Each shot is drawn by an artist who may see that shot alone.

The examples in these rules show the kind of thing meant. Do not reuse them.

Honesty. Breaking one of these makes the ad false:
- Only claims the product's page makes. A number (time or money saved) is said only if the page says it, and exactly as the page says it.
- On screen, the product does only what it really does.
- No reviews, no testimonials, no quotes from customers, no "#1", no guarantees.
- The owner in the story is made up, so tell it as a story about them ("Dana runs a hauling yard"), in the narrator's voice. Never have a made-up owner say what the product did for them. That is an invented review.

The shape, in this order:
1. The problem, in the first 3 seconds: mid-action, specific, felt. This is the opening.
2. What it costs: the job lost, the hour gone, the customer walking away.
3. The product arrives, and the narrator names it.
4. The product doing its most impressive real feature, on its screen, in at least 2 shots.
5. The result: what is different for the owner now. This is the `payoff`.
6. "We can build one for your business too."
7. The last shot is the call to action: link in bio.

The narrator:
- A warm storyteller, never the owner.
- Speaks from the first second (the first shot has a line) and in at least three of every four shots.
- Says the product's name when it first appears, and Wright AI Solutions once, near the end.
- Short plain words, as if telling a friend about someone they know.
- The first line says who this is and what their problem is, in plain words ("Dana runs a hauling yard, and her phone rings all night"). Every line follows from the one before.
- Ears-only test, checked by code: listeners who hear only the narration must say what it's about, who everyone is, and what happened.

The drawings (`visual`):
- One action per shot, caught mid-move, that one drawing can show.
- Every `visual` stands on its own: who is in it, where they are, what they are doing, with what, and what their face shows.
- Each character has one thing on or by the head that tells them apart (a hard hat, a headset, a bun), and every `visual` names them by it.
- Name the real objects of the workplace, specifically enough to draw them.

The product's screen:
- It is shown big and readable in at least 2 shots, as an insert of the phone or laptop with the feature working.
- Give the exact words on the screen, in quotes: at most three short labels of a few words each ("Missed call: Ray's Roofing", "Booked: Tue 9:00").
- Show the feature finishing its job, not a menu or a logo.

The camera:
- `camera` is exactly one of: wide, medium, close-up, over-the-shoulder, low angle, high angle, insert.
- Change the camera on every cut, and use at least three kinds. The product's screen is always an insert.

The caption:
- It names the product, says we build custom AI agents and software for businesses, and ends with "Link in bio", then 3 to 5 hashtags.

A bright, confident flat-vector film still. Each shot is watched on a phone for two or three seconds, so it has to read at a glance: who is here, where they work, and the one thing that is happening.

Characters:
- White stick figures: stroke #f5f5f5, stroke-width 12 to 18, round caps and joins, with simple expressive faces (eyes, brows and a mouth that show the feeling).
- Arms bend at the elbow and legs at the knee, so the pose itself shows the action.
- Each is told apart by one thing on or by the head.

The workplace:
- A real workplace (a truck yard, an office, a kitchen, a shop floor), drawn in full, edge to edge, with depth: a floor in perspective, walls behind, and 2 or 3 layers where far things are smaller and darker.
- Real detail that says what kind of business this is, with every object the shot names recognisable from its outline.

Light:
- Warm light from a window or from overhead lights, with a soft shadow under every person and object.

Screens (phones and laptops):
- Drawn big, clean and readable: a white or dark card with rounded corners, filling most of the space between y 330 and y 1250 in an insert.
- The feature is shown as simple blocks, icons and short labels at least 48 px tall, with the words exactly as the shot gives them.
- One thing on the screen is the point. It is the brightest thing in the frame.

Palette:
- Backgrounds in dark slate (#111827 to #334155), with the warm light on top.
- Lime #a3e635 for the product's moment of success and for its buttons. Nothing else is lime.
- Red #ef4444 only for the problem: a missed call, an error. Nothing else is red.
