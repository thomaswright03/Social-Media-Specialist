# Format: current-event explainer (entertainment)

One piece of news from the last 14 days explained fast with bold motion
graphics. Every fact has a dated source. It never sells.

<!-- ideas -->
Format: a current-event explainer. Entertainment: no selling, no product, no business.
What wins: one specific, surprising piece of news from the last 14 days that affects ordinary people or small businesses (a new law, a price change, a tech launch, a record, a strange court case), framed as a question the viewer wants answered ("Why is your grocery bill about to go up?"). Pick news you can confirm from at least 2 dated sources; skip anything you can't date.
A pitch is one sentence that states the news and the open question, with no jargon.
<!-- /ideas -->

<!-- script -->
Format: a current-event explainer. Entertainment: no selling, no product, no business.
- A narrator explains it out loud from the first second (the first shot has a line) and speaks in every shot: confident, plain words, short sentences, no jargon. With the sound on, the voice alone explains it.
- Structure: the surprising fact (0 to 3 s), what happened (who, what, when), why it happened, what it means for the viewer, and one last surprising detail as the payoff.
- 10 to 14 shots, 2 to 3 seconds each. Each shot shows ONE graphic that proves the line: a big number counting up, a map zooming in on a place, a before-and-after bar, a timeline with one date lit, an icon of the thing (a phone, a factory, a courthouse), a simple stick-figure presenter pointing at it.
- Every number and claim is on screen as it's said ("onScreen") and comes from a source in "sources". At least 2 sources, each with its link and its date (YYYY-MM-DD), all from the last 14 days. Never invent a number; if a source doesn't say it, leave it out.
- Cameras: use them as graphic moves: wide (the whole map or chart), close-up (one number), insert (one icon), high angle (a map from above). Never the same camera twice in a row.
- "place" is the graphic space ("the map", "the chart", "the newsroom"). One or two.
- The caption says what happened in one line, credits the sources by name, and asks for an opinion ("Good idea or bad idea?"), plus 3 to 5 hashtags.
<!-- /script -->

<!-- style -->
Bold editorial motion graphics, like a sharp news explainer: a deep navy-to-black background (#0b1020 to #121a33) with a faint grid, big flat shapes, thick clean lines, and simple icons built from geometry. Numbers and labels drawn huge in a heavy sans-serif look (thick strokes, at least 120 px for the key number). Charts and maps are flat with 2 or 3 colours: white for the base, lime #a3e635 for the thing that matters, red #ef4444 only for a fall or a danger, a muted blue-grey (#64748b) for everything else. When a person is needed, a white stick-figure presenter (stroke #f5f5f5, 14 px, round caps, simple face) pointing at the graphic. Depth from layered cards with soft drop shadows, not from perspective scenes. Motion: things slide in, count up, draw on and highlight, each on a crisp ease-out.
